# Neural-value search promotion gate

## What changed

The existing `metaexitr1` checkpoint remains the policy and transformer backbone. A
separate 463,625-parameter value head reads the frozen transformer's player-view
history embedding and estimates eventual terminal win probability. The base model
has 142,832,563 parameters and is never updated by this training run.

Interior search states must enter the transformer through
`InformationSetTransformerProjection`. That bridge exposes only copied primitive
player observations, validates sampled-world lineage, strips mechanical successor
objects, and hides private continuations from `repr`. `TransformerValueOracle`
refuses uncertified mechanical objects and pins both model hashes.

## Data contract

`terminal_value_v1` contains 57,475 point-of-view trajectories from 28,729
battles: 23,639 human POVs, 28,838 historical search/self-play POVs, and 4,998
league POVs. Splits are deterministic and battle-disjoint (22,983 train, 2,845
validation, 2,899 test battles). The training sampler uses 40% human, 40%
self-play, and 20% league examples. Labels are actual terminal wins/losses, not
search scores or teacher preferences.

This prevents the same battle from appearing on both sides of the evaluation. It
does not eliminate population shift, duplicated strategies, or correlation among
battles generated by one policy; the source-stratified test audit addresses those
risks separately.

## Evidence obtained

On the battle-disjoint test set, the chosen epoch has Brier 0.178634, log loss
0.526373, and 72.06% POV accuracy. The constant-base-rate reference is Brier
0.250020, log loss 0.693188, and 49.69% accuracy.

The battle-weighted audit over all 2,899 held-out battles reports:

| Slice | Accuracy | Brier | ECE (10 bins) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Overall | 71.78% | 0.17984 | 0.0131 |
| Human | 70.55% | 0.18971 | 0.0146 |
| Self-play | 73.77% | 0.16674 | 0.0235 |
| League | 69.74% | 0.18902 | 0.0404 |
| Early 0-25% | 58.65% | 0.23913 | 0.0173 |
| Mid 25-75% | 70.35% | 0.19410 | 0.0219 |
| Late 75-100% | 86.96% | 0.09710 | 0.0346 |

This proves independently held-out terminal outcome signal exists in R1's
representation. It does **not** prove that neural-guided search chooses better
actions.

## Fail-closed promotion protocol

The promotion protocol now requires at least 100 paired units, 50 roots, and 50
distinct source battles. For each unit, run both arms under the same declared 500
ms wall-clock cap and score both actions against a frozen 187,000-iteration oracle.
Schedules are averaged within root, roots are averaged within source battle, and
bootstrap resampling is performed only over source battles.

The candidate in this gate is an eight-trial, policy-weighted one-step
information-set search. Nonterminal certified successors are evaluated by the
transformer value head, terminal successors use exact outcomes, and the resulting
action value is blended with the frozen R1 prior. This is an equal-cap comparison
against the existing R1-prior root MCTS, not a pure leaf-evaluator swap inside the
same MCTS implementation.

`experimental.src.eval.neural_value_root_gate` rejects promotion unless all are
true:

1. at least 100 paired units from at least 50 roots and 50 source battles declare
   500 ms and stay within 5% wall-clock overrun;
2. at least 5% of candidate leaf evaluations are certified neural leaves;
3. the lower endpoint of a paired-bootstrap 95% interval for oracle-regret
   improvement is above zero; and
4. oracle top-1 agreement is no more than two percentage points below baseline.

Only a pass authorizes a counterbalanced 100-game head-to-head. Only a subsequent
game-level pass authorizes 25k-100k PFSP/league generation.

## Executed result: do not promote

The semantic continuation bridge now covers ordinary moves, hard switches, forced
switches, public items, abilities, boosts, and already-existing field state while
remaining fail-closed on unaccounted engine instructions and terastallization. On
the expanded 69-root census, 63 of 1,104 depth-zero trials reached a certified
nonterminal continuation (5.71%) and four reached an exact terminal (0.36%). Ten
trials survived to a second certified nonterminal transition (0.91%) and two to a
second exact terminal (0.18%). There were no policy-support, common-support,
lineage, tracker, or terminal-consistency failures; the remaining failures were
semantic projection rejections. This is descriptive one-sided coverage, not a
performance claim.

### Correction to the first transformer root result

The 69 transformer roots were later audited and found to be turns from one source
battle (two player views of the same battle), not 69 independent battles. The old
root-clustered bootstrap therefore understated correlation and is not a promotion
gate. The rows remain a descriptive fixed-state diagnostic: the candidate used
certified neural leaves for 1,200 of 19,104 leaf evaluations (6.28%), stayed within
the timing cap, and was much worse on that panel:

| Metric | Baseline | Candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Oracle top-1 agreement | 70.29% | 45.65% |
| Mean oracle regret | 0.00418 | 0.02999 |

The candidate changed the action in 53 of 138 units. No confidence interval or
independent-population claim is attached to these correlated rows. The gate schema
was bumped to v2 and now rejects panels with fewer than 50 source battles.

### Controlled public-value leaf swap

A separate 18-feature value MLP was trained on actual terminal outcomes from 4,998
historical self-play battles. Its inputs are determinization-invariant: opponent
reserve HP, moves, items, abilities, EVs, and stats are never read. The 3,329-
parameter model reached battle-weighted test Brier 0.18761 and 70.04% accuracy on
733 held-out battles, but reduced search throughput by about 35% and failed its
60-battle gate (90.0% baseline versus 80.0% oracle top-1).

Its training states are historical trajectory decisions rather than sampled
search-interior counterfactuals. The evaluator can consume interior public states,
but the experiment therefore includes a deployment-distribution shift and cannot
learn the outcome of actions that were not taken.

A frozen latency adaptation trained a 449-parameter 16x8 MLP with validation Brier
0.18740. It was tested on a second, non-overlapping 60-battle panel. Throughput loss
fell to about 13.7%, and the neural value was used on 36.94% of search iterations,
but the independent confirmation still failed:

| Confirmation metric | Baseline | 449-param candidate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Oracle top-1 agreement | 87.50% | 77.50% |
| Mean oracle regret | 0.00118 | 0.00386 |

Paired regret improvement was -0.00268 and the source-battle-clustered bootstrap
95% lower endpoint was -0.00503. The candidate was better on 2 paired units, equal
on 104, and worse on 14. The controlled leaf-value branch is therefore stopped;
neither H2H nor league generation is authorized.

Both value heads and both negative gates should be preserved. Further value work
would require action-conditional or counterfactual targets that distinguish nearby
choices; another scalar terminal-outcome model or blend-weight sweep is not
justified by these results.

The 187,000-iteration oracle is a frozen high-budget version of the existing
hand-evaluated search, not a terminal-game ground truth. Agreement with it is only
the predeclared screen for authorizing an H2H. Two screen failures reject this
candidate without claiming that oracle regret is itself ladder win rate.

### Action-conditioned root-Q follow-up

A separately frozen local experiment generated schedule-averaged values for every
legal action at 1,000 battle-independent roots using 800 million stock-MCTS
teacher iterations. A 13,441-parameter public/action MLP reached 69.17% pairwise
ordering accuracy on 96 validation battles, but only 32.29% best-action agreement
and 0.05643 mean regret. The preregistered admission thresholds were 35% and 0.03,
respectively, so the model was stopped before the independent root gate.

This is narrower than a rejection of counterfactual values. It rejects the
compact 176-feature action representation: the targets contain useful ranking
signal, but the model loses too much matchup and history context at the top-action
boundary. A successor requires a separately declared richer representation; the
untouched root-gate population must not become a tuning set.

A second preregistered representation experiment joined 998 of the same roots to
their exact schema-v3 R1 observations and cached the accepted frozen transformer's
900-dimensional deployed stateless embeddings. A 235,797-parameter 13-action Q
head fit training strongly (70.71% top-1, 0.00581 regret) but generalized poorly:
40.54% validation top-1 and 0.05467 regret versus 53.21% and 0.04310 for the
historical 500 ms action. It therefore failed admission before the root gate.

The train-validation gap rejects a bigger head as the immediate fix. Production
R1 intentionally uses a stateless two-step prior because live action/reward
boundaries can be missing. A truly history-conditioned Q experiment first needs a
verified causal history/RL2 contract; otherwise offline history gains would not be
deployment-aligned.

## Research basis and 95% GXE

The architecture follows the learned-policy/value plus search pattern of
[Expert Iteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.08439) and
[Student of Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03178), with the public-information
discipline motivated by [ReBeL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.13544). The
[Metamon paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.04395) supports sequence models,
terminal critics, and diverse human/self-play data in Pokemon battles; it also
reports that same-policy self-play can fail to transfer consistently to humans.
These papers support the experiment, not a guaranteed effect size in Pokemon.

At rating deviation 25, moving from the settled 92.4-92.7 GXE range to 95 GXE is
approximately a 75-point stabilized Glicko rating gain. No defensible conversion
exists from the held-out value-head metrics to that ladder gain. A sustained result
near 60% against R1-strength opponents is roughly a 70 Elo signal, but ladder
population transfer must be measured. Consequently, 95 GXE is a program target,
not the expected result of this head alone.
