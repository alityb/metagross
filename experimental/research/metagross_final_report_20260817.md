# Metagross: Final Report

**A policy transformer grows overconfident as observed history accumulates
in its context — not, it turns out, because it reads its own choices;
every serving-time correction traded robustness for decisiveness, whose
value has opponent-dependent sign — so head-to-head evaluation cannot
license deployment, and we built the population gate that can.**

## The finding chain

Five results, each generated by the previous one:

1. **The question.** The same 142.8M network served two ways — reading the
   battle history versus only the current state — plays *near parity*:
   dead even head-to-head (48% over 200 games; see the integrity note), a small stateless
   edge on the public ladder (91.7 vs 89.4 GXE at matched RD-25). Two
   puzzles are born: why would history hurt at all, and why do our two
   measurements disagree?
2. **The mechanism.** Only the history-reading view collapses to
   overconfidence: prior entropy falls ≈0.96 → ≈0.58 nats by turn 30+
   while the *same weights* served stateless stay calibrated on the *same
   states*. Replicated twice. The preregistered ablation then refuted the
   obvious explanation: masking the agent's own past actions removes only
   ≈10% of the collapse. The driver is observed-history *volume* itself —
   collapse grows monotonically with context length, and even a 5-step
   window carries 2.4× the stateless collapse. Not self-conditioning:
   history-induced sharpening.
3. **Two principled fixes, two informative failures.** Fixing the decision
   rule (Gumbel-style completed-Q) lost 10-40: pooled Q across sampled
   worlds re-commits the strategy-fusion fallacy, revealing that visit
   counts were doing robustness work, not just recording exploration.
   Fixing the calibration (entropy-matched temperature flattening) passed
   every controlled test — 55.5% over 200 games against the
   champion — then lost ≈8 GXE on the real ladder.
4. **The recognition.** The flattening result and the original
   parity-vs-gap discrepancy are the same phenomenon: head-to-head and
   population measurements *flip signs*, observed twice in one campaign.
   Interventions trading decisiveness for robustness pay against strong
   systematic opponents and bleed against the weak, erratic majority.
   There is no opponent-independent "well-calibrated" prior. The
   consequence is an instrument: a frozen-league population gate — built,
   calibrated, and retrodiction-tested: under its frozen promotion rule it
   rejects flattening, agreeing with the ladder where the head-to-head
   screen said ship. A third instance then confirmed at power: the
   champion's own priors measure 44.0% [37.3, 50.9] against the identical
   no-prior search stack, while carrying it to rank #105 on the ladder.
5. **Why the chain has this shape.** In states where sampled worlds
   disagree, expert humans match the majority world 36%, a minority world
   31%, and *no sampled world at all* 33% — chance-level across the board.
   Human play under ambiguity is not expressible as "solve worlds, then
   pick among their answers"; robust cross-world aggregation is precisely
   the capability our interventions kept trading away.

The agent itself: to our knowledge the strongest reported agent for
`gen9randombattle`, playing at elite human level — settled **92.4–92.7 GXE
at RD 25** (peak 93.6), **reproduced months later at 91.7** on a fresh
account under a formal protocol. **Rank snapshot (2026-08-28, hashed in
`ladder_rank_snapshot_20260828/`):** both measurement accounts sit on the
public gen9randombattle top-500 leaderboard at the time of writing — the
stateless arm at **rank #105** (Elo 2249, GXE 91.2) and the causal arm at
**rank #246** (Elo 2182, GXE 89.3) — placing the agent's accounts inside
the top ≈0.1% of an active ladder whose #1 sits at 95.5 GXE. No verified
bot leaderboard exists, so the claim is "strongest *reported*," not "best
in format." It remains unbeaten by anything this program built —
including three systematic improvement programs whose disciplined negative
results are the evidence behind links 1–5.

## Prologue — the agent, and the two programs that ruled out the obvious

The deployed agent is `randbats_exit_r1` — one accepted expert-iteration
round on top of Metamon's offline-RL policy — guiding root-only PUCT search
through 8–32 sampled hidden-team worlds at 500 ms per decision. Before the
chain above could be asked cleanly, two full programs eliminated the two
obvious improvement axes:

**Search deeper?** The expert-iteration program (Appendix A) spent ≈48
preregistered cycles trying to demonstrate *any* slow expert stronger than
production search: terminal-MCTS controllers, one-deviation controllers, an
equal-prior 8,192-iteration searcher, counterfactual target bridges, a
causal interior prior. Every family failed its prospective live gate, and a
key saturation fact emerged: 8,192- and 20,000-iteration search agree on
86.95% of top-1 actions (repeat JSD median 5×10⁻⁵). Depth is not the
binding constraint.

**Infer better?** The belief program (Appendix B) measured hidden-set
inference directly: one real win (the reveal-filter v2, +4.7 mass), three
clean nulls, and a ceiling — beyond consistency filtering, gen9 randbats
hidden sets carry little externally-inferable signal (within-set human
move choice is near-uniform at 1.33 of 1.39 nats). Sharper inference is
not the missing capability either.

What remained was the serving path itself — and a question nobody had
asked with matched rigor.

## Link 1 — The question: does history conditioning help live play?

Two fresh accounts, same machine, same deterministic 472k-iteration search
budget, single variable = trajectory mode, both converged to the Glicko
RD-25 floor — the exact standard of every historical number:

| Serving mode | Record | GXE | Glicko ± RD |
|---|---|---|---|
| causal-history | 149-81 | 89.4 | 1902 ± 25.0 |
| legacy-stateless | 174-83 | 91.7 | 1952 ± 25.0 |
| (reference) frozen-r1 causal | 141-85 | 86.6 | 1851 ± 25 |
| (reference) historical stateless era | — | 92.4–92.7 | RD 25 |

The historical 92.4 substantially reproduces (91.7, fresh account, months
later). The corrected causal path reproduces r1 and lands slightly above
it. And the puzzle: the modes sit ≈2 GXE apart at population level while a
direct 200-game head-to-head measured 48% — parity. The six-point historical gap
decomposes into mostly era difference, a small real population edge, and
no head-to-head edge at all. At this point the discrepancy was noted but
not yet recognized as a phenomenon; link 4 returns to it.

A secondary quantification worth recording: the causal-history repair
changes R1's top action on 32% of decisions, yet the ladder moves ≈2 GXE —
the search layer absorbs large policy perturbations. Search is an
equalizer.

## Link 2 — The mechanism: the history-reading view miscalibrates itself

The causal prior's entropy collapses from ≈0.96 to ≈0.58 nats by turn
30+ — measured offline on the 699-decision paired corpus, then replicated
independently in cloud games (0.98/0.73/0.72/0.58 by turn bucket, ≈550
decisions). The stateless prior stays flat. Same checkpoint, same battle
states; the only difference is whether the network reads the trajectory —
which, late in a battle, consists substantially of its own past choices.

The obvious story — the network reads its own past confident choices and
doubles down, the game-playing analog of LLM self-conditioning — was our
leading hypothesis, and the preregistered ablation
(`selfcond_ablation_20260828`, thresholds frozen before any number was
seen) **refuted it**. Offline replay of the frozen 699-decision corpus at
exact parity (sanity 0.0 abs diff, 735/735 rows):

| Condition | Collapse C (nats, bucket 0-9 minus 30+) |
|---|---|
| full causal | 0.388 |
| truncate K=5 / 10 / 20 / 40 | 0.223 / 0.227 / 0.238 / 0.310 |
| **own-actions masked, full length** | **0.351** |
| stateless reference | 0.093 |

Masking the agent's own actions removes only ≈10% of the collapse
(ratio 0.905 ≥ the frozen 0.66 threshold for H-length). The driver is the
*volume of observed history itself*: collapse grows monotonically with
window size, and no truncation depth approaches the stateless profile —
even 5 steps of context yields 2.4× the stateless collapse on identical
states. The honest mechanism claim is therefore **history-induced
sharpening**, not self-conditioning; the natural-experiment design (same
weights, same states, history dosage varied) is what made the refutation
cheap. Practical corollary: truncation (gate C) can only partially remove
the pathology — the flat-entropy serving mode is stateless itself.

## Link 3 — Two principled fixes, two informative failures

The mechanism suggested two training-free corrections. Both were
implemented under the campaign's activation discipline (absent env =
byte-identical serving; malformed = fail-closed; active = one observable
log line, mandatory for validity).

**Fix the decision rule — Gate B, decisively harmful.** Gumbel-style
completed-Q (`log π(a) + scaled Q̂(a)`, Danihelka et al.'s
evaluation-time rule) should yield policy improvement under an
overconfident prior. It flipped 37% of decisions and lost **10-40**
against the unmodified visit rule. The reading: completed-Q is proven
where Q comes from many visits in *one true world*; under determinization,
pooled Q averages over inconsistent hypotheses, and acting greedily on
averaged values is the classic strategy-fusion fallacy re-emerging at the
decision-rule level. Visit counts are implicitly fusion-proof — they
concentrate only where many sampled worlds independently agree. The visit
rule's variance discipline is load-bearing.

**Fix the calibration — Gate D, the campaign's deepest result.** An
entropy-matched temperature schedule (offline-calibrated on the paired
corpus) produced three valid measurements that appear to conflict and do
not:

1. Mechanism: the schedule verifiably restores late-game search breadth
   (activation-proven telemetry; it overshoots the entropy target).
2. Head-to-head vs the stateless champion: **111-89 (55.5%, CI
   [48.6, 62.2])** over 200 games — a ≈7-point recovery over the
   plain-causal baseline in the same matchup.
3. Ladder population: **≈81.7 GXE vs the 89.4 plain-causal reference** —
   roughly eight points of harm, persistent across ≈139 games,
   promotion-excluded by direct calculation before the run finished.

The same serving-time entropy, with zero opponent adaptation anywhere in
the intervention, was positive-EV against one strong systematic opponent
and negative-EV against the population. Search breadth is not a
calibration parameter with a correct value; its optimum depends on who
created the position. "Well-calibrated" is undefined without a population.

## Link 4 — The sign flip, and the instrument it forces

Twice in one campaign, the two measurement families disagreed in
*direction*: causal-vs-stateless is parity head-to-head but a gap on the
ladder; flattening wins head-to-head and loses badly on the ladder. The
consistent account: interventions that trade decisiveness for robustness
pay against strong systematic opponents and bleed against the weak,
erratic majority of a real population. Neither measurement is "the truth";
they answer different questions — and only the population answer licenses
deployment. This rule is now binding on all promotion decisions.

**Direct confirmation (2026-08-28, from on-disk ladder protocol logs):**
stratifying every ladder game by opponent Elo at match start shows the
predicted structure exactly. Against strong opponents (1600+) the three
configurations are statistically indistinguishable; flattening's entire
deficit concentrates below 1600:

| Opponent Elo | flattened | plain-causal | stateless |
|---|---|---|---|
| below 1600 | 34-16 (68.0%) | 29-3 (90.6%) | 31-10 (75.6%) |
| 1600+ | 52-30 (63.4%) | 121-74 (62.1%) | 145-81 (64.2%) |

Flattening plays weak opponents like strong ones — the decisiveness it
trades away is what converts winnable games against the sub-1600 majority.
The mechanism behind the sign flip is measured, not inferred
(`strength_stratified.json`).

The instrument is a frozen-league population gate
(`experimental/src/scripts/league.py` + `league_20260826/PREREGISTRATION.md`):
any candidate serving configuration plays mirrored pairs against a fixed
pool spanning strength and style (stateless peer, plain-causal
self-mirror, heuristic no-prior foul-play, weak hyper-aggressive), and is
graded on the *per-opponent vector* — promotable only by weak dominance
over the plain-causal reference vector, with the weak-opponent cells as
the exploitation guard. Per-matchup activation checking, port-gated
candidate interventions, idempotent resume, and stall watchdogs are built
in — each from a recorded failure.

Status: both league runs are complete (the first locally
verified-mirrored games of the project). Reference vector (plain-causal):
62.5% vs stateless / 45.0% self-mirror / 41.7% vs vanilla foul-play /
91.7% vs max-damage. Retrodiction (flattened, identical seeds): 47.5 /
45.0 / 33.3 / 91.7 — weakly dominated, so under the frozen promotion rule
the league **rejects flattening, agreeing with the ladder where the H2H
screen said ship**. Graded PARTIAL per prereg: the deployment-relevant
half lands; the 55.5% H2H win did not reproduce at 40-game power (CI
contains both values). Two bonus measurements: the first direct
flattened-vs-plain-causal H2H (45.0%), and a striking reference cell —
the champion's priors went 10-14 against the identical no-prior search
stack — a lead the preregistered 200-game test (`priors_h2h_20260828`)
then confirmed: **88-112 (44.0%, CI [37.3, 50.9])**. No detectable
head-to-head edge, upper bound at parity, against a leaderboard-verified
7–10 GXE population gap. The sign flip's third and cleanest instance:
the policy guidance that carries the agent to rank #105 is worth, at
best, nothing against its own unguided search stack head-to-head. Its
entire measurable value is exploitation of the population.

Cheaper complements, in order: the offline paired-decision pre-gate
(exists), AIVAT-style luck-corrected anytime-valid scoring (cf. AV-AIVAT,
arXiv 2608.06362) for order-of-magnitude game reduction with certified
stopping, and strength-stratified ladder analysis when a ladder run is
warranted at all.

## Link 5 — Discussion: why the chain has this shape

The Gate-A corpus (Appendix B) holds the explanatory result. Where the 8
posterior worlds' searches agree, humans and R1 both match deep search
≈55–60%; where ≥3/8 worlds disagree, both collapse to 30–39% — and in
those ambiguous states the human action matches the majority world 36%, a
minority world 31%, and *no sampled world* 33%. Expert human play under
ambiguity is not "pick the likely world and play its best response"; it is
not expressible in the solve-worlds-then-choose family at all. Combined
with the belief ceiling, the weight of evidence points at **robust
cross-world aggregation** as the capability that separates human play from
this architecture class — the same robustness that visit counts partially
provide (link 3a), that flattening traded away (link 3b), and that no
single-opponent evaluation can price (link 4). The literature's largest
recent effects sit exactly there: MAPLE-style shared-tree aggregation
(2605.24139) and dynamic determinization budget allocation (2607.13007).

### Integrity note (2026-08-28): the "mirrored" label on local screens

A root-cause investigation of a deterministic harness hang surfaced a
second, larger finding: mirrored-team enforcement required an environment
variable on the *Showdown server's* side that local screen launchers never
set. Artifact-level verification (actual battle teams vs registered
manifests; e.g. 0/6 sampled pairs mirrored in the prediction-2 v2 screen,
all registration files unconsumed) confirms the affected local screens
played ordinary independent random-team games. Consequences, stated
precisely: the results **stand** — they were analyzed as unpaired Bernoulli
games, which is what they were, and side-alternation (client-side) was
unaffected — but the "mirrored" descriptor on those runs is wrong, and the
designed variance reduction silently never happened, which is why several
SPRT screens ran to their caps undecided. The harness now fails any game
whose team registration was not consumed, and the league runs are the first
locally verified mirrored measurements. This is lesson 4 (observable
activation) recurring one layer down: *enforce consumption, not
registration* — every mechanism needs proof it fired, in the artifact it
was supposed to affect.

## Methodology the project paid for

1. **Prove the expert live before distilling.** Honored throughout; it is
   why 48 cycles produced no false success and nothing was ever distilled
   from an unproven expert.
2. **Offline success fails live, repeatedly.** Locally calibrated
   corrections, equal-prior deep search, LCB-gated re-solving, and
   direct-policy gates all looked better offline than they played.
3. **Head-to-head success fails at population level** — the campaign's
   addition to (2), observed twice with a mechanism. Population gates
   license deployment; H2H only diagnoses.
4. **Observable activation, or the result is void.** It took five attempts
   to measure prediction-2 validly (a silently inert hook, a wedged cloud
   container, a resume bug, and a dual-arm contamination caught by a
   zero-activation-lines audit). No intervention result is valid without
   proof in the logs that it was on — and off for the control.
5. **Search-internal confidence does not identify safe corrections.**
   Found independently by Cycle 41 and the July selective re-solving
   record.
6. **Evaluation design is a first-class experimental object.** A
   "symmetric" scorer measured 44% for a self-pair; counterbalancing
   exposed a 10-point slot effect; an interim peek voided a cycle.
   Mirrored pairing, SPRT, outcome-blind watching, and fail-closed data
   admission were each earned from a specific recorded failure.
7. **Infrastructure needs liveness *and* progress checks.** Three separate
   runs were wedged by the same class of silent post-game hang (one billed
   ≈40 idle hours); a deterministic reproduction is preserved and under
   root-cause. Watchdogs now check progress freshness, not just process
   existence.
8. **Close threads with decisions; cheap gates make honesty affordable.**
   Undecided gates compound into folklore. Every thread this program
   touched now ends in a measured verdict or a preregistered stop.

## Appendix A — The expert-iteration program in full

### The objective (frozen 2026-08-15)

1. A slow controller must prospectively beat frozen production R1/search in
   paired H2H with a confidence interval excluding 50%.
2. Only then, one Expert-Iteration/distillation round.
3. The distilled system must survive retesting at an equal 500 ms budget.

Offline accuracy, target stability, positive point estimates, and unpowered
canaries were preregistered as non-successes.

### The negative chain (all preregistered, all hash-frozen)

| Candidate expert | Result | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Terminal-MCTS recurrent controller | 72–78 / 150 | FAIL |
| Randomized one-deviation controller (early) | below frozen gate | FAIL |
| Equal-prior 8,192 root controller (Cycle 41) | 11/20 vs ≥13/20 gate; 165/575 overrides; internal confidence did not identify safe corrections | FAIL |
| One-deviation attribution reruns | Cycle 42 VOID (interim-look breach); Cycle 43 integrity-fail (Struggle semantics), protocol-retired | RETIRED |
| Counterfactual depth-one target bridge (Cycle 47) | 5.42% support, 8/64 battles | REJECTED |
| Causal interior prior, Gate A (Cycle 48) | 498/512 states admitted (97.3% row coverage) but <512 unique fingerprints; 80 fail-closed causal-integrity rows | FAIL — architecture stopped per program |

Cycle 48's failure was concentrated in three reproducible mechanics-tail
contracts (item-consumption authority, late-battle PP authority, R1 served-
action vocabulary). Its conditional positives — 86.95% top-1 agreement
between 8,192- and 20,000-iteration equal-prior search on observed states,
repeat JSD median 5×10⁻⁵ — establish that the search target itself is
highly reproducible; the expert built from it simply was never shown to be
stronger.

**Exhaustion finding (2026-08-16):** with every admitted family closed, the
controller-first / distill-second objective was declared exhausted on
current evidence rather than continued through repair cycles. MegaGem's
lesson survived intact in procedural form: *nothing was distilled from an
unproven expert at any point in this project.*

## Appendix B — The belief program in full

On 2026-08-16 the owner redirected the assets: improve the deployed agent,
outside the MegaGem framing. The archive audit surfaced two July threads
abandoned mid-gate; finishing them and mining the new Gate A corpus set the
agenda.

### Corpus findings (Gate A artifacts, 498 observed states)

1. **Disagreement with deep search is a belief-ambiguity phenomenon.**
   Where the 8 posterior worlds' searches agree unanimously, humans and R1
   match deep search ≈55–60%; where ≥3/8 worlds disagree, both collapse to
   30–39%. Search depth is not the binding constraint.
2. **Humans do not follow the majority world.** In ambiguous states the
   human action matched the majority-world recommendation only 36%
   (minority world 31%, *no* sampled world 33%).
3. **R1 has a confident-miscalibration class:** 83/498 states across 46
   battles where the human matches deep search but R1 confidently
   disagrees (median 0.61 on its own pick vs 0.16 on the human+search
   action).

### The Skat literature import

Buro et al. (IJCAI-09), Solinas/Rebstock/Buro (AAAI-19), and Policy
Inference (CoG-19) define the exact template: bias determinized-search
world sampling by likelihoods learned offline from human play, evaluated by
TSSR (how much likelier than uniform the true hidden state is sampled).
The project's rematerializer supplied what July lacked: true hidden teams
as **held-out evaluation labels** (never agent inputs), enforced by file
boundary.

### One day of offline gates (13,365 decisions, 248 held-out battles, $0)

| Belief candidate | Result |
|---|---|
| Generator-pool prior | mass 0.366 / top-1 0.432 (avg ≈3.5 candidate sets/species) |
| + moves-reveal filter (production equivalent) | mass 0.541 / top-1 0.589 |
| **Filter v2: + item/ability/tera reveals** | **+4.7 mass / +5.0 top-1 on the complete key space, at every reveal depth — PASS** |
| First-revealed-move evidence tables (107k instances) | null (top-1 −0.8); leaky-smoke lift was memorization |
| Learned action likelihood, shallow context (392k rows) | val NLL 1.3305 vs marginal 1.3318 — ≈ marginal; dead |
| Marginal-likelihood TSSR arm | +0.001 mass — null; closes the family |

**Conclusion:** gen9 randbats hidden-set inference beyond consistency
filtering carries little externally-inferable signal at shallow context —
candidate sets are few, reveal quickly, and within-set human move choice is
near-uniform given shallow context (1.33 of 1.39 nats). The belief
posterior is near its practical offline ceiling at filter v2. Corollary:
corpus finding 2 is more plausibly about **aggregation and robustness
across worlds than about sharper inference**.
